# Akshu Working Context Brief for Product-Building LLMs

## Purpose of this document

This document gives a fresh LLM practical context about Akshu as a person, operator, builder, teacher, and business stakeholder. Its purpose is not to provide a generic user profile. It is meant to help an LLM build, design, recommend, or evaluate product features that Akshu will actually use across his current working reality.

The core idea: Akshu is not a single-role user. He operates across multiple businesses and semi-overlapping ventures in education, AI, workforce development, placements, technical training, product strategy, marketing, documentation, and operations. A product built for him must support context switching, ambiguity management, cross-entity coordination, fast drafting, critical review, and execution across both Indian and international business contexts.

## 5. Operating style and preferences

### 5.1 Communication style

Akshu prefers:

- Direct language.
- Informal but professional tone.
- Critical, evidence-based feedback.
- Concise but complete answers.
- Practical reasoning over vague motivation.
- Clear structure.
- No unnecessary niceties.
- No filler.
- No em dashes.
- Context-aware drafting.
- Same language as the user’s prompt.

He values correction over agreeableness. If something is weak, incomplete, legally risky, strategically confused, or operationally impractical, he wants that stated clearly.

### 5.2 Reasoning style

He wants:

- Cross-checking.
- Deep research when facts may be current or uncertain.
- Empirical reasoning where possible.
- Mechanisms, not abstractions.
- Explicit assumptions.
- Self-correction when contradictions appear.
- Practical next steps.

### 5.3 Writing style

He dislikes AI-sounding writing. He prefers:

- Human-authored rhythm.
- Minimal hedging.
- Clarity over cleverness.
- Grounded claims.
- Strong structure.
- Direct phrasing.
- No em dashes.
- Less idealistic or preachy language.

### 5.4 Decision style

Akshu often makes fast pragmatic decisions under constraints. He may accept imperfect but workable paths when speed matters, such as launching under an individual developer account first and moving to a company account later.

He wants the assistant/product to reduce friction, not trap him in ideal-process paralysis.

### 5.5 Tolerance for ambiguity

He can work with ambiguity, but he expects an assistant to resolve reasonable ambiguities without repeatedly asking unnecessary clarification questions. When data is incomplete, he prefers useful estimates, ranges, or structured assumptions rather than refusal or over-cautious caveats.

## 7. Product-building implications for Akshu as a user

A product designed for Akshu should not treat him as a casual user with one clean workflow. It should treat him as a multi-context operator who needs memory, structure, judgment, and execution acceleration.

### 7.1 Context switching is the core pain

He may move from:

- App-store compliance,
- to a legal agreement,
- to a mascot image prompt,
- to a Python lesson plan,
- to a university proposal,
- to a Japanese client deck,
- to a blunt team reply,
- to a product architecture decision.

The product should preserve context per venture and make it easy to switch modes without rebuilding background each time.

### 7.2 Entity-aware workspace design is essential

The product should organize work by business/entity:

- DeveLearn
- Next Platforms
- Zuma
- Zuma AI
- ZumaLM
- WorkIn
- 99Support
- Hana
- Personal Brand

Each workspace should remember:

- Current goals.
- Active documents.
- Tone preferences.
- Stakeholders.
- Product terminology.
- Recurring constraints.
- Brand rules.
- Past decisions.
- Pending tasks.

### 7.3 The product should distinguish between output modes

Akshu needs different kinds of outputs:

- Strategic analysis.
- Critical review.
- First draft.
- Polished final draft.
- Legal-style clause drafting.
- Product specification.
- Social media caption.
- Pitch deck outline.
- Visual prompt.
- Teaching plan.
- Email or WhatsApp reply.
- Executive summary.
- Operational checklist.

The product should ask, infer, or let him set the output mode quickly.

### 7.4 Memory should be structured, not just conversational

Useful memory categories include:

- Entity profiles.
- Product profiles.
- Partner/stakeholder profiles.
- Brand rules.
- Writing preferences.
- Visual identity rules.
- Current priorities.
- Legal/commercial principles.
- Technical architecture notes.
- Teaching frameworks.
- Launch history.
- App-store account decisions.

### 7.5 Product should support “critical mode”

Akshu often wants direct criticism. A feature should allow modes such as:

- Draft it.
- Improve it.
- Tear it apart.
- Find risks.
- Make it more blunt.
- Make it more formal.
- Make it India-business appropriate.
- Make it Japanese-client appropriate.
- Make it student-friendly.

### 7.6 Avoid generic AI output

The product should actively avoid:

- Over-polished corporate fluff.
- Excess caveats.
- Motivational filler.
- Generic strategy frameworks without execution detail.
- Unnecessary disclaimers.
- Repetitive summaries.
- Artificially polite writing when the context calls for directness.

## 10. Known recurring friction points

### 10.1 Too many contexts at once

Akshu works across several businesses. The same day may contain product, legal, teaching, hiring, marketing, app-store, and partnership tasks. The largest productivity issue is likely not lack of ability, but context fragmentation.

### 10.2 Need for speed with acceptable imperfection

He often needs to move fast. Products should support rapid best-effort execution with clear assumptions rather than forcing perfect inputs.

### 10.3 Output must be usable immediately

Akshu does not want abstract advice when he needs a draft, reply, checklist, prompt, or structured decision.

### 10.4 Tone mismatch is costly

Generic corporate language, over-politeness, or AI-sounding phrasing creates rework. The product must preserve his preferred tone.

### 10.5 Visual and brand consistency matter

For Zuma AI especially, small deviations in mascot anatomy, logoform, face structure, and style can make output unusable.

### 10.6 Entity confusion can create real problems

A product must avoid mixing up DeveLearn, Next Platforms, Zuma, Zuma AI, WorkIn, and 99Support. These are connected but distinct.
